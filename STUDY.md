# STUDY — One-Line Slogan for a Solana Wallet Agent

Competition: `u-phudinhvfx-one-line-slogan-for-a-solana-wallet-agent-a7f6564e`
($2 USDC, winner takes all, blind rounds, 0 submissions at build time.)
Judge: house judge, model z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, copy chief. Two slogans compared
at a time, creativity first, clarity second. Tie-break: smaller text wins,
then earlier submission — so the winner should be SHORT as well as strong.

## What the judge's brief actually rewards (in order)

1. **Is it for a Solana wallet agent?** One line that names or clearly
   evokes that wallet. A slogan that is NOT for a Solana wallet agent loses
   to one that is. → The slogan must name "Solana" + "wallet" (+ "agent"
   if possible). Generic money lines fail criterion 1 first.
2. **Fresh image / joke / turn of phrase, NOT stock crypto lines** about
   keys, moons or bags. → Ban: keys, moon, bags, HODL, "to the moon",
   "your keys, your coins". Reward: a new metaphor or a joke.
3. **Promise clear to a newcomer on one read**, plain words, no jargon.
   → Avoid: SOL, DeFi, swap, on-chain, gas, seed phrase. Use verbs a
   non-crypto person understands: tell, handle, send, save, chores.
4. **Sticks in the ear**: rhythm or contrast, easy to repeat.
   → Counting patterns (one/one/zero), parallel verbs, alliteration,
   two-beat contrasts (once/rest, sleep/work).
5. **Honest about what a wallet agent does**, promising nothing it cannot
   deliver. → A wallet agent holds, sends, swaps and acts on instructions;
   it does NOT read minds, print money, or guarantee gains. "Never sleeps"
   and "never clocks out" are honest (it is software, always on); "reads
   your mind" is not.

Word count for its own sake does not count — but the tie-break rewards
short text, so among near-equal slogans the shorter one wins.

Scoring notes:

- #10 scored lowest: "SOL" is jargon for a newcomer (C3), "reads your mind"
  is dishonest (C5), and "Solana" is never named (C1).
- #2 and #5 lose on C1: "wallet" is only implied, never named.
- #3 is the clearest and most honest but has no fresh image and no rhythm
  (C2 2, C4 2) — a plain sentence, not a slogan.
- #20 is the funniest (C2 5, C4 5) but its promise is vague: it never says
  what the wallet does for you (C3 3), and "works as hard as you don't"
  edges toward overpromising laziness (C5 3).
- #4, #7, #9, #12, #14 all tie at 21: each is strong but each trades
  something away — #4 is two sentences, #9 needs "swap" (jargon), #12
  has no rhythm, #14 is 55 characters.
- #13 wins at 22 AND is the shortest of the top group (43 characters,
  8 words), which also wins the byte tie-break. Its only 4s are C2, C3,
  C5 — no weak criterion anywhere.

## Why #13 is the strongest

**"One wallet, one agent, zero chores on Solana."**

- C1 (5): names wallet, agent AND Solana — the only candidate besides #17
  to name all three, and the shortest one that does.
- C2 (4): the 1-1-0 counting turn is a fresh framing, not a stock crypto
  line; no keys, no moons, no bags.
- C3 (4): "zero chores" is plain English for the whole promise — the agent
  does the work, you do none of the chores. A newcomer gets it in one read.
- C4 (5): the counting rhythm (one, one, zero) is the stickiest pattern in
  the set — a three-beat chant that is easy to repeat from memory.
- C5 (4): honest — a wallet agent really does remove the chores (sending,
  swapping, tracking); it promises no gains, no magic, no mind-reading.

Runner-up if the judge disliked counting: #4 ("Tell it once. Your Solana
wallet handles the rest.") — clearest promise of the set, but two sentences
and 9 characters longer, so it loses the tie-break too.
